Best Rodent Proofing Methods for Amarillo Properties in 2026

Published 2026-11-18 · Updated 2026-11-18 · By Amarillo Rodent Control

Rodent proofing comes down to size and materials. Mice and rats use gaps smaller than most people expect, and the wrong filler gets chewed straight through. Here is what Extension sources say works, and where to start on an Amarillo property.

How small is too small

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension describes a mouse entry as a hole the size of a dime, or a crack about 1/4 inch tall under a door. For rats it is a hole the size of a quarter, about 1/2 inch. UC IPM advises sealing every opening larger than 1/4 inch, because young rats and mice can squeeze through less room than you would guess.

Source: Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, Rodent IPM; UC IPM, Pest Notes: Rats

Materials that hold up and materials that fail

Rodents chew. UC IPM says plastic screening, rubber, vinyl, foam, and wood are not effective barriers against mice, since they can chew through them. Steel wool can plug a hole for a while but may rust. Metal and concrete are the lasting fixes.

  • Galvanized hardware cloth over vents and larger openings
  • Sheet metal or metal flashing at gnawed corners and edges
  • Concrete or mortar for foundation cracks and gaps around pipes
  • Door sweeps and tight weatherstripping on garage and entry doors

We pair metal with a durable exterior sealant so the patch stays tight where wind and sun keep working on it.

Source: UC IPM, House Mouse

Where to look first on an Amarillo home

Start at the ground and work up. Check where pipes and lines enter at the foundation, cracks in slabs and block walls, garage door corners, crawl space vents, weep holes in brick, and dryer and exhaust vents. Then move to the roofline, including soffits, fascia, gable vents, and utility lines.

Older homes tend to have more openings after decades of settling. Newer homes often have gaps around HVAC lines and utility penetrations that were never sealed during construction. Our rodent exclusion services page explains how a full perimeter job is sequenced.

Seal after the rodents are out

Sealing keeps new rodents out, but it does not remove the ones already inside. Close the openings only after the animals are gone, or you can trap them in the walls. That is why proofing usually follows trapping and cleanup. UC IPM calls exclusion the most successful and permanent form of control, and it adds that sanitation alone seldom controls mice.
