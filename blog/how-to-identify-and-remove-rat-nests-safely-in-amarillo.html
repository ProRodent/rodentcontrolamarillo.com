How to Identify and Remove Rat Nests Safely in Amarillo Homes

Published 2026-11-18 · Updated 2026-11-18 · By Amarillo Rodent Control

Finding a rat nest is a sign the rats have been settled for a while. Here is how to recognize one, why you should not disturb it casually, and how the CDC says to clean up safely.

What a rat nest looks like

Rats build nests from soft material such as shredded paper, insulation, fabric, and plant matter. UC IPM says roof rats usually nest in enclosed, elevated spaces such as attics, wall voids, ceiling voids, and cabinets. Norway rats burrow along foundations and under rubbish or woodpiles, and UC IPM also lists firewood stacks, boxes, and garage drawers as places nests turn up.

Source: UC IPM, Pest Notes: Rats

Why not to disturb one

Rodents can carry hantavirus and shed it in droppings, urine, and saliva. DSHS says the virus can spread in the air on dust particles, and the safest approach is to avoid stirring up dry nest material. A nest that has been disturbed can also scatter rats into new parts of the house.

Source: Texas DSHS, Hantavirus fact sheet

Cleanup steps from the CDC

  1. Open doors and windows and stay out of the area for 30 minutes.
  2. Wear rubber or plastic gloves. For heavy infestations, the CDC also calls for disposable coveralls, goggles, and a HEPA respirator.
  3. Spray the nest and any droppings with disinfectant or a fresh bleach solution until very wet, and wait 5 minutes.
  4. Do not sweep or vacuum. Pick up the material with paper towels.
  5. Bag it, knot the bag, seal it inside a second bag, and place it in a covered trash can.
  6. Disinfect your gloves, remove them, and wash your hands.

Source: CDC, How to Clean Up After Rodents (breeding and nesting habits)

When to leave it to a professional

Nests in attics, wall voids, and crawl spaces are hard to reach and often come with heavy contamination. Our rat nest removal service removes the nest, disinfects the area, and finds the entry point that let the rats in. Sealing that opening is what keeps the next nest from forming.
