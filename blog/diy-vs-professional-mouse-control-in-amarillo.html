DIY vs Professional Mouse Control in Amarillo: What Works Best Locally?

Published 2026-11-18 · Updated 2026-11-18 · By Amarillo Rodent Control

A few mice and a free weekend can be a fair DIY project. A steady stream of mice is usually a sealing problem. Here is where DIY works, where it stalls, and what changes when a professional gets involved.

What DIY can handle

For a new, small problem, UC IPM suggests setting traps close to walls, behind objects, in dark corners, and wherever you see activity. In active areas, traps can sit about 10 feet apart, and setting two traps 1 to 2 inches apart can catch mice that jump over obstacles. Take away food and clutter, and seal gaps larger than 1/4 inch with metal.

Source: UC IPM, House Mouse

Where DIY tends to stall

Mice multiply quickly. NC State Extension says they can start breeding at about 6 to 8 weeks of age, gestation lasts about three weeks, and a female can have 5 to 10 litters a year. If one gap stays open, trapping can keep pace with the problem without ever ending it.

Material choice matters too. UC IPM lists foam, plastic, rubber, vinyl, and wood among materials mice chew through. A gap you filled with foam in spring can be open again by fall.

Source: NC State Extension, Biology and Behavior of Common Structure-Invading Mice; UC IPM, House Mouse

Signs it is time to call

  • Traps keep catching mice week after week with no drop in activity
  • You still hear scratching in the walls even though traps are clear
  • Droppings keep showing up in new places
  • Sealing the gaps you can reach has not stopped it
  • You find a nest or a large amount of droppings

What a professional adds

A technician inspects the whole structure, including the roofline and utility lines that are hard to reach from the ground. The visit also identifies the species, seals the openings with metal, and handles cleanup with the right protection. Our mouse infestation treatment and mice control services pages explain each step, and the mouse proofing and sealing entry points page shows how sealing is done.

If you are not sure whether you are at the DIY stage, a free inspection will tell you.
