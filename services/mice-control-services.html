Professional Mice Control Services in Amarillo, TX

House mice are among the most commonly reported rodent pests in homes, and they are hard to eliminate for good without sealing the entry points. A mouse can enter through a gap about 1/4 inch tall, roughly the width of a dime, according to Extension sources. In older housing, gaps like that are easy to find: aging pipe boots, settling foundation joints, dishwasher supply line gaps, and dryer vent openings that were never sealed.

Amarillo sits in farm country, with grain handling and feed operations around the city, which can give field mice food and cover close to town. Fall harvest season, roughly September to November, is when many homeowners first notice mice, because cooler weather and changing fields send them looking for shelter.

The Amarillo Mouse Problem: Local Context

Mouse pressure in Amarillo does not follow a single season. Summer heat can send mice indoors for cooler spaces and water, cold snaps can push them toward warm buildings, and spring storms can drive them out of damp nesting spots. Mice can breed year-round indoors, so every seasonal change can bring a new reason for them to move in.

Neighborhoods along Amarillo's western and northern edge, including Greenways, River Falls, and Sleepy Hollow, are the most exposed to field mouse activity in the fall. Homes near these areas should complete exclusion work in September before harvest season peaks.

How We Identify a Mouse Infestation

The most missed sign is sound. Scratching and rustling in the walls late at night is often an early sign, and many homeowners put it down to the house settling for weeks before they find visible evidence. By the time droppings turn up under the kitchen sink, the infestation has usually been going for a while.

Our inspection uses UV lighting to detect urine trails that are invisible to the naked eye and maps the full extent of mouse activity. That shows which areas are actively used and which have only old evidence, which matters for trap placement.

Our Mice Elimination Protocol

Phase 1: Activity Mapping UV inspection maps all active runways. Droppings are counted and located to establish a baseline density estimate. We distinguish between high-activity and peripheral zones.

Phase 2: Intensive Trapping Snap traps are placed along walls at confirmed mouse runways, where mice tend to travel. We use enough traps to keep up with the population.

Phase 3: Population Monitoring We return every 5-7 days to clear catches and track activity. Two consecutive visits with no new catches tell us the colony is gone.

Phase 4: Complete Exclusion Every identified entry point is sealed with a material suited to the gap: galvanized mesh for openings, commercial caulk for pipe penetrations, and door sweeps for settling exterior doors. Our mouse proofing service is the step that prevents the next infestation.

Phase 5: Sanitation Guidance We advise on sanitation improvements for your property, such as secure food storage, fewer outdoor attractants, and less moisture, to lower the risk of re-infestation.

Mouse Control for Amarillo Apartments and Rentals

Amarillo's student housing near West Texas A&M University, the rental market in Oliver Eakle and surrounding areas, and the older apartment stock throughout central Amarillo can all see mouse problems linked to shared walls and deferred maintenance. Treating one unit at a time often fails in multi-unit buildings because mice move to untreated neighbors. Our building-wide approach treats the whole structure as one job.
