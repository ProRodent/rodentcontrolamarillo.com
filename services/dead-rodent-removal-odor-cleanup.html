Dead Rodent Removal and Odor Cleanup in Amarillo, TX

A strong smell under the house, in the ducts, or in a bathroom wall is one of the most common reasons people call. Often it is a rodent that died somewhere you cannot reach. The odor comes from decomposition, and it stays until the source is found and removed.

Not every bad smell is a dead animal. Sewer gas, mold, and spoiled food can smell similar. Our inspection looks for rodent evidence first, and if we do not find any, we tell you so instead of treating for something that is not there.

How We Find and Remove the Source

Smell mapping. We follow the odor through the house, then check the likely spots: wall voids, attic spaces, the crawl space, and accessible areas around ducts and vents.

Access and removal. When we locate the animal, we open only what we need to reach it. Removal is done with gloves and appropriate respiratory protection, and the remains are double bagged and disposed of.

Disinfecting and deodorizing. Affected surfaces are disinfected, and we treat the area to neutralize the source of the odor instead of masking it with fragrance.

Finding out how it got in. A rodent that died inside came in somewhere. We look for the entry point and seal it, so the problem does not repeat. The sealing work follows our rodent exclusion process.

If the Smell Is Coming From the Ducts

Odors that seem to come from vents often start nearby, such as a wall or attic space the ductwork passes through. We inspect the accessible areas around the ducts. If contamination has reached the duct system itself, we will say so and suggest the right contractor for duct cleaning.

Safety First

The CDC advises ventilating a space before cleanup, wearing gloves, wetting droppings and nesting material with disinfectant before removal, and never sweeping or vacuuming it. If the animal is in a wall, an attic, or a crawl space, it is usually safer to call than to open the space yourself. See also our rodent droppings cleanup and attic cleanup pages.

Where Dead Rodents Usually Turn Up

Rodents tend to die in places that are hard to see. The most common spots are wall voids, attic spaces, the crawl space, and the cavities around plumbing and ductwork. Smells that seem to come from a vent or a single room often start one wall or one floor away, which is why a guess at the location can send you in the wrong direction.

Callers describe it as a smell under the house, a smell coming out of the ducts, or a smell like a dead rat in a bathroom. Whatever the description, the first step is the same: an inspection to find where it is coming from.

What You Can Do Before We Arrive

  • Note where the smell is strongest and what time of day it is worst.
  • Do not spray air fresheners into walls or vents, because it hides the clue we need.
  • Keep children and pets away from the area, and do not try to pull the animal out yourself.
  • If you saw droppings or heard scratching earlier, tell us, since it helps identify the species.

After the Source Is Removed

Once the animal is gone and the area is treated, the smell usually improves as the area dries and airs out. If the rodent had been living in the space for a while, there may also be droppings or nesting material to clean. Our technician will tell you what is needed and what is not.

Why a Dead Rodent Is More Than a Smell

The odor is the part you notice, but it is not the only issue. A dead rodent can attract flies, and the nest, droppings, and urine that came with it stay behind. The CDC notes that rodents can carry diseases such as hantavirus, which is why remains and nesting material should be handled with gloves and wetted with disinfectant first, never swept or vacuumed.

What Happens During the Visit

  1. We ask where the smell is strongest, when it started, and whether you have heard or seen rodents.
  2. We inspect the likely areas, starting with the spot you describe and working outward to the attic, crawl space, and exterior entry points.
  3. If we find the animal, we explain how we plan to reach it before any wall or panel is opened.
  4. We remove it, disinfect the area, and treat the source of the odor.
  5. We seal the entry point and check for any live rodents still in the building.

Pets and Bait Stations

If we find that live rodents are still active, we place tamper-resistant bait stations and traps where pets and children cannot reach them, and we tell you exactly where each one is. This matters because a pet that eats a poisoned rodent can also be harmed, a risk Texas A&M AgriLife Extension specifically warns about.

Preventing a Repeat

Rodents that die in walls usually got in through a gap that is still open. Sealing with metal mesh and durable sealant, as described on our rodent exclusion page, is what stops the next one. Many homeowners also book a yearly maintenance inspection so new gaps from weather and settling are caught early. Call (806) 779-3064 to schedule.
